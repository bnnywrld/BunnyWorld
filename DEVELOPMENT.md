# Developing BunnyWorld 0.2.1.14

Godot 4.5.1 Standard, GL Compatibility. Fresh campaigns only. Provinces are movement tiles and unique-building sites; state populations/demographics are independent of province count. No save migration.

## Monthly economy and public services (0.2.1.14)

The user-approved first release is Phase 1 plus basic automatic trade. Construction stays fully paid upfront; ordinary budget changes apply next month, independently of Agenda laws. Electricity remains an inactive framework. Education/healthcare quality and gradual employment were explicitly added to this phase. Older sections below describe their respective earlier implementation stages.

`economy.gd` owns national budget/quality/productivity/CPI/food-stress state, global prices, monthly commodity ledgers and bilateral settled flows. `WorldState` keeps existing starting GDP/person × owned population plus staffing-scaled completed-building value added; Economy applies real productivity, relative employment and input-availability factors, then a separate CPI for nominal GDP. Starting factors are one. Moving existing industries into construction does not remove their starting value added, and trade proceeds are not added to GDP a second time. Actual real growth samples constant-price output after monthly population/workforce updates. Deposit capacities, building inventories and starting money/population are unchanged. Unknown starting fiscal balances use zero only when the first budget needs an opening cash/debt value; initial unknown metadata is preserved.

Simulation uses calendar-month boundaries. WorldState interleaves daily construction/research/Agenda with crossed month updates, preserving quality effects on subsequent research days. Society advances one month at a time: Economy applies pending budgets, quality, productive capacity, jobs, production/trade/prices, food stress, CPI and public finances; population grows/declines; capacity and employment snapshots rebuild; Economy finalizes real-GDP growth and emits UI changes. Direct batched month advances follow the same sequence. All country processing has a stable sorted order. No simulation or market work runs on idle frames.

`build_economy_rules.py` reproduces the tunable ten-good/building catalogue and agricultural potential. It reads MD-derived construction/staffing metadata plus the existing province-ID atlas and ESA-2000 prepared terrain types. Cosine-weighted cropland-family coverage supplies state potential; neither terrain nor geography is modified. Cropland plus staffed agriculture capacity calibrates national baseline farm output, allocated to source states by crop/farm weights. State employment loss constrains baseline farm/extraction output; state food/resource capacity follows its current owner. Initial global food supply is calibrated to 108% of need. Explicit Agriculture Districts add capacity above their starting staffed baseline; no starting district is counted twice. Initial industrial/extraction output coefficients calibrate global source capacities to benchmark demand plus the same supply margin. This is gameplay balance, not measured production or a physical RU-to-tonne conversion.

RU remains MD deposit capacity. MU is a standardized monthly bundle, with Food specifically one person-month basket. Six source resources produce extraction flows; staffed civilian industries, chip/composite plants and refineries supply their relevant goods. Available civilian factories manufacture; construction factories keep construction activity. Aggregate national input demand follows GDP demand shares; recipe dependency availability constrains supported output. Recipe quantity shares, energy, operating-cost and technology fields are reserved for later sector-specific refinement, not an individual company simulation. No stockpile or detailed military-equipment production is introduced. `construction_panel.gd` adds a visible gold-bordered manufacturing card above the queue with live factory assignment and last-settled output.

The market allocates domestic production first, then affordable import demand with Food first. A national private purchasing envelope is 55% of monthly GDP. Global scarcity apportions affordable requests before partner matching; multiple sellers are allowed and no seller can export its domestic allocation. Buyers rank eligible surplus suppliers by directional opinions, outlook and proximity, with lexical tie breaking. Rankings review every six calendar months or on opinion/outlook changes; opinion decay is sampled at the review boundary so reloads and batched dates reproduce the same results. `trade_access` is the attachment point for future sanctions/agreements/transport; these currently have no effect. Quantity and money totals conserve across bilateral flows. A single USD/MU benchmark changes monthly by supply/demand pressure, capped at ±2.5%, with configured floor/ceiling bounds. Last-settlement prices are saved separately from the new benchmark, so recorded values match their actual price. Export proceeds stay business activity; government gets GDP-based population/corporate taxes, not the entire export value.

Budgets store two taxes plus seven spending categories. Taxes apply to calibrated 62% population / 38% corporate income bases; category spending is annual GDP percentage paid one twelfth monthly. Active Taxation laws cap editable tax rates (20/35/60%). Pending settings apply/clamp next month. Interest begins at 2.5% with debt/GDP and fiscal-stress increments, capped at 25%; deficits exhaust cash then borrow exactly the remainder, without an instant-collapse threshold. Surpluses stay in Treasury; manual repayment is bounded by available cash/debt. Construction payments/refunds retain existing cash/borrowing accounting, are recorded separately, and are never debited a second time by budget settlement.

Quality is a shared 0–100 Economy-owned value. Initial MD education/health spending-tier ideas map to provisional quality scores; GDP/person supplies a bounded fallback. These are gameplay seeds, not measured education/health indices. Maintaining quality q costs `1 + 5q/100` percent GDP for education and `1 + 7q/100` for healthcare. Active funding defines a bounded target; each month quality moves 2% toward it with a ±0.6-point cap. Research daily work and current-rate ETA use `(0.5 + education/100) × (0.9 + 0.1 sqrt(R&D funding percent))`, in addition to the declining ahead-of-time penalty. ETA holds current quality/funding constant; future changes can alter it. Productivity gains 1.2% plus up to 0.8% annually from education. Healthcare adds `(q−50) × 0.01` annual percentage points to the shared 1% base population rate. Food stress moves 15% toward unmet-need severity each month and can subtract up to six annual percentage points; severe sustained shortages can therefore cause decline. Surplus adds no growth bonus. Signed population deltas retain fractional residents and save/load correctly; chart proportions stay fixed.

Employment follows MD's aggregate-sector approach, including its civilian-industry coefficients with dependent retail/service jobs and its separate agriculture/resource sectors. Existing employed residents are preserved initially: tracked buildings provide explicit jobs, and the remainder belongs to baseline services/farms/other businesses. Monthly demand follows activity and added building requirements; actual hiring approaches demand by 12%, with a frictional unemployment floor. Participation/inactivity do not change. `Demography.state_workforce` reuses exact age allocation while avoiding religion/education chart construction in staffing updates; immutable age-share caches key state and retirement age. Aging/profile changes must invalidate that cache when later implemented.

UI uses the existing shared panel and button theme. Economy has Overview/Budget/Production/Energy; Production also opens from its toolbar shortcut. Budget edits survive routine UI refresh until submitted; controls apply as one user action. Research shows quality/speed/maintenance funding, Society shows both qualities/trends, and reusable styled rich population tooltips cover the top bar, national/state inspectors, Society, demographics and world ledger. Monthly totals/modifiers are green/red. Trade shows all ten goods, current/settled prices, domestic/import/export/unmet quantities and actual partner flows. Electricity/compute, technology-specific economic effects, military/public-service benefits, migration/approval, organization bonuses, sanctions/manual deals, monetary policy, cycles and crises remain explicitly inactive attachment points.

Save schema 29 persists pending/active budgets, qualities/trends, productivity/CPI/jobs/food stress, interest/budget records, construction cash-flow summaries, global/settled prices, bilateral flows, commodity balances and up to 120 months of price history. Validation checks exact field/catalog identities, finite/bounded values, inactive future systems, monthly timestamps, quantity balance, matched imports/exports and settlement value/price consistency before any mutation. Load rebuilds derived aggregates without repricing saved GDP. Tests cover initial calibration, financial conservation, timing, quality/research/growth, shortages, gradual employment, upfront payment/no double charge, strict corruption rejection, save/resume and batched/sequential determinism, decade balance, native UI and smaller-screen layouts. No old-save migration.

MD resource-sector/subsistence/public-service employment formulas are not transplanted.

Construction allocations retain their physical factory counts; effective work multiplies by national civilian-industry staffing fulfillment. Each completed building records its quoted full-staff GDP contribution by building type in `construction_output`; GDP queries multiply it by the current state/type staffing fulfillment. Existing starting GDP remains the calibrated baseline, avoiding a second repricing of the imported economy. New output changes with staffing; construction itself creates no automatic Treasury income or hires from unemployment. Save schema 27 validates per-type output records and their sum against the full-staff ledger before mutation; staffing is rebuilt from saved population, workforce rates and buildings.

Government uses the original large 230×132 minimum parliament; country summaries stay compact. Seats are ordered by angle across rings into exact-count party wedges. Polar seat cells are unioned once per layout/data change into group borders; hover highlights the whole party and shows its support/seats. Cached polygons redraw without rebuilding on hover. The next-election line derives the first date after MD's last election plus its month interval; an elapsed date is marked Due, never rolled forward as if a vote occurred. Election simulation remains inactive. Currency labels have an explicit minimum height and unlimited wrapping so their name is visible.

The diplomatic dock loads all catalogue rows, uses an internal scroll and category filter, and retains the collapse arrow/owner lifetime. MD scripted actions, influence-window actions and inherited standard actions are represented; AI attaché aliases merge with their ordinary action. Every button is disabled, with no PP, opinion, resource or diplomacy effect.

Validation: `tests/state_economy_tests.gd`, `tests/state_economy_ui_tests.gd` and the current construction/political/society/research/model/stability suites. QA saves and rendered captures stay outside the checkout. Source data/imports change no cartography or packed assets.

## Research (0.2.1.9)

`research.gd` owns national completed technologies, retained technology progress, university assignments and pending completion notifications. Each existing institution is one slot; any institution may work in any field. Prerequisite paths are alternative predecessors, explicit dependencies are additional requirements. Duplicate active research is rejected. Choosing another project replaces the assignment but retains all work nationally, so another institution can resume it. Only the player runs research; other countries retain their imported starting unlocks.

The simulation advances at midnight through WorldState, including every day in a batched advance. One day's work is `1 / (1 + max(0, days_until_tech_year)/365 * ahead_factor)` base research days. MD supplies `BASE_TECH_COST = 250`, each technology's multiplier, and `BASE_YEAR_AHEAD_PENALTY_FACTOR = 1`. The penalty is recalculated daily; future dates do not block selection. Institution ETA forecasts this same daily integration instead of freezing the current penalty. Completion applies no bonuses/equipment/economy effects, releases the institution and adds one saved notification without pausing. Dismissing a notification removes it permanently. No research queues, funds, idle-slot banking, country modifiers or special-project simulation are enabled.

`research_panel.gd` uses the inherited buttons and shared navy/gold styles, a scrollable category TabBar and a two-axis scroll canvas. Source coordinates choose branch lanes; topological chronological rows put predecessors above successors, including same-year steps. One source dependency points from a 2005 prerequisite to a technology dated 2000; its card stays below that prerequisite and retains its actual source year. `technology_canvas.gd` draws static prerequisite connectors. Category changes rebuild one category; research signals refresh the visible cards and institution status. No research work is done in frame callbacks. The window closes on selection, Escape, × or owning-tab changes. `research_notifications.gd` displays up to three dismissible cards plus a remaining count using the briefing frame; it never pauses the clock.

`python tools/import_research.py /path/to/bunnyversion.zip` reproduces `data/research.json`; see DATA_SOURCES.md for branch selection and import boundaries. Save schema 24 validates all technology IDs, initial unlocks, finite/in-range progress, unique valid institution assignments, prerequisites and notification timestamps before changing live state. Completed technologies, paused work, active assignments and undismissed notices survive save/load; older saves require a fresh campaign.

## Construction (0.2.1.8)

`construction.gd` owns the player-only queue via a weak reference to WorldState. Entries lock their construction-point quote and cash/borrowing split on purchase. Allocation walks queue order, at most 10 factories per job, from the current owned civilian-industry count; the remainder is labeled Manufactured Goods. No goods units/stockpile are fabricated. Completed jobs update state building counts and add their annual GDP output exactly once. Ownership losses pause affected jobs; existing progress can still be cancelled. Newly completed factories enter capacity the next day. Simulation uses daily batches; no queue work occurs while paused or empty, and a completed job's excess daily points are not carried between jobs.

MD's base construction speed is 4 points/factory/day. The first scaffold adapts the MD .75 infrastructure coefficient to a +7.5% speed bonus per level, excluding infrastructure itself. It does not implement technology, law or sector modifiers. Monetary prices are MD billion-USD values from the supplied building pricing table; construction-point costs and per-level extra costs are separate fields. Refunds return cash × unfinished fraction and reverse borrowed × unfinished fraction, with any already-repaid borrowing returned to cash. Queueing and cancellation never add GDP. Infrastructure/Internet/Railroads cap at 10 with independent slots. Other caps/shared/coastal rules follow MD. Existing counts above a normal per-type cap are retained as the minimum allowed cap; current networks are already within ten levels. Electrical-grid specializations are mutually exclusive for new construction.

Capacity = floor(2 + 5 ln(1 + area_km²/250) + 8 ln(1 + population/50,000)), with a floor of existing shared buildings + max(4, ceil(existing × .25)). Capacity is seeded once on fresh campaigns, reserves queued shared buildings, and never shrinks beneath an existing city layout. This is provisional game balancing, not an MD limit or physical land-use model. It gives large and populous states substantial room while keeping small states compact.

GDP retains the original country GDP/person × current owned population and adds completed state output. MD base per-building billion-USD coefficients are multiplied by the owner's starting overall_productivity/1000 (neutral sector modifiers, full staffing); network/energy direct contributions use the MD constants. Full staffing is a scaffold assumption: employment, resource inputs, energy constraints, productivity growth and macroeconomics are not enabled. GDP accessors feed country summaries, top bar, ledger and GDP map modes. Construction/manufacturing assignments do not remove an existing factory's GDP. Added output follows state ownership; it is not deposited in Treasury.

`construction_panel.gd` uses shared navy/gold styles, a building picker, bounded 30-entry queue pages, current/wanted factory counts, progress/ETA, priority and refund controls. `main.gd` dispatches state clicks without replacing the tab. Private map mode 8 disables the normal picker while open, saves the previous 0–7 mode, and restores it on close, Escape, tab switch or loading. States stay visible; province/city clutter is hidden. The shader uses an 8192×1 R8 queue flag texture (8 KiB) and a screen-space yellow stripe calculation only in Construction mode. No geography/membership assets or meshes are regenerated. Slot reservation caches rebuild once per queue topology revision; progress-only ticks refresh the UI without palette uploads.

`tools/import_construction.py /path/to/bunnyversion.zip` reproduces `data/construction.json` from the supplied MD building/money scripts. It derives coastal states offline from existing province/sea masks, excluding inland water, and writes no map files. The state Railroads entry deliberately uses MD rail_terminal as a state-network abstraction. Province rail routes/ports/forts, new Data Centers and research facilities remain separate future work.

Save schema 23 persists queue IDs, locked quotes, payment split, factory preferences, progress, completed buildings, state capacity and added GDP. Validation checks ownership tags, capacities, limits, coastal rules, finite amounts and valid queue identifiers before assigning any live state. No old-save migration.
